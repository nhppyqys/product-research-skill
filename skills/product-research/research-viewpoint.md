# Viewpoint

**Settle this before collecting anything.** The same evidence supports four different reports, and a report that serves everyone serves no one.

⚠️ **Field failure:** a report was delivered with no viewpoint decided. It described the product accurately and answered nobody's question — the buyer couldn't tell whether to buy, the founder couldn't tell whether to build, the investor couldn't tell whether it was a good business.

**If the request doesn't state a viewpoint, ask. One question, then proceed.** Guessing wrong wastes the entire round.

## The four viewpoints

| Viewpoint | The question they're actually asking | Written in full | Kept brief |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Buyer** | Should we buy this, and what will it really cost? | Pricing teardown, terms, support quality, migration cost, billing complaints | Funding, valuation, capital efficiency |
| **Founder** | Is this market worth entering, and where? | Landscape layering, gaps, moats, capital efficiency of incumbents | Purchasing process, contract terms |
| **Investor** | Is this a good business? | Revenue quality, retention, capital efficiency, distribution structure, category growth | Feature comparison, UI detail |
| **Product manager** | What should we learn or copy? | Feature architecture, user journey, friction points, release cadence | Funding, valuation |

## What every viewpoint gets regardless

Six things are never trimmed, whoever is reading:

1. **The jargon-free opening summary**
2. **What it is, in business language** — feature modules, not endpoints
3. **How it makes money** — billing unit above all
4. **Who actually uses it** — with named evidence where it exists
5. **What users say** — verbatim, with dates
6. **Boundaries** — what wasn't obtained and how that limits the conclusions

## Writing the verdict

**The verdict section is split by viewpoint and each part is directive, not descriptive.**

- **Buyer** — who it suits, who it doesn't, and *what to confirm before signing*. Concrete: ask about the discount, get the cancellation process in writing, check whether the add-on is mandatory.
- **Founder** — whether to enter and *where to cut in*. "Don't enter head-on" is a valid answer; so is "the gap isn't in features".
- **Investor** — what the fundamentals support and *which numbers need pressure-testing before valuing it*.
- **Product manager** — what's worth copying and what the friction points reveal.

⚠️ **A verdict that only describes is not a verdict.** "The product is mature with a strong feature set" tells no one what to do.
